01

Research

My research asks how interfaces can be designed so molecules, polymers, catalysts, and solids behave in useful new ways. I move between synthesis, surface modification, characterization, and reaction testing, with a habit of turning messy interfacial questions into measurable design rules.

The near-term scientific center is selective CO2 hydrogenation to ethanol and higher alcohols. I am especially interested in catalyst architectures where metals, oxides, and organic or polymeric components cooperate through local interfaces rather than acting as isolated ingredients.

CO2 to ethanol

Designing multicomponent catalysts that turn carbon dioxide into ethanol and higher alcohols with better selectivity, activity, and mechanistic clarity.

AI for materials chemistry

Using data, model-guided screening, and chemical intuition to accelerate materials discovery without losing sight of experimental reality.

Automated laboratories

Building toward closed-loop experimentation where synthesis, reaction testing, characterization, and analysis can learn from each other quickly.

Semiconductor surface chemistry

Exploring etching, deposition, ALD, and surface modification as precise tools for controlling chemistry at technologically important interfaces.

Cooling-interface corrosion

Studying corrosion and erosion where water-cooling systems meet metals, coatings, and flow-driven interfacial stress.

Polymer nanoconfinement

Quantifying how pore size, surface chemistry, and confinement reshape polymer dynamics, degradation, viscosity, melting, and gas uptake.
